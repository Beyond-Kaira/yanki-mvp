# Passwords people actually choose — the blocklist NIST SP 800-63B asks a
# verifier to keep. Loaded once at import by `app.services.password_policy`.
#
# FORMAT. One entry per line. Blank lines and lines starting with '#' are
# ignored. Entries are stored in the CANONICAL form the policy reduces a
# candidate to before looking it up: lower-cased, NFKC-normalized, leet-folded
# ('@'->a, '0'->o, '3'->e, '$'->s, ...). Do not add an entry with mixed case, a
# digit substitution or a trailing year — the canonicalizer already strips
# those, so 'P@ssw0rd2024!' finds the entry 'password' on its own. Adding the
# decorated form instead is dead weight.
#
# WHY IT IS SHORT. This is a curated head of the distribution — the base words
# that dominate every breach corpus — not a dump of one. It is small enough to
# read and to justify line by line, and the canonicalization above is what
# makes it cover far more than its own length: one 'password' entry rejects
# every 'Passw0rd!', 'p@ssword123' and 'PASSWORD2026' that would otherwise
# clear a length rule. Growing it to a full top-100k list from a maintained
# source is an ops task, logged as tech debt — the loader does not care how
# many lines it reads.
#
# The Turkish section is here because this product's users are: a blocklist
# built only from English corpora is blind to exactly the passwords its own
# customers pick.
