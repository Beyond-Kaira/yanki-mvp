## What shipped

P8.3, whole, in two halves across two pull requests.

**PR #33 — the API half** (`574c19a`, `44f57f7`). The backlink engine had
existed since session 21 (five tables, the `BacklinkSource` seam, a
deterministic mock, authority/toxicity/delta logic) and had no route to it at
all: it was reachable from tests and from nothing else.

- `backend/app/services/backlinks.py` (+525) — the service layer between the
  engine and HTTP.
- `backend/app/api/backlink_routes.py` (+414) — **12 route handlers**:
  `/summary`, `/referring-domains`, `/anchors`, `/events`, `/opportunities`,
  `POST /refresh`, `/competitors` (list, create, delete = 3), `/export.csv`,
  `/disavow.txt`. Registered in `app/api/main.py`, mounted under
  `/api/v1/seo-projects/{project_id}/backlinks`.
- `backend/app/api/backlink_schemas.py` (+226).
- `backend/tests/test_backlink_api.py` (+596).
- `shared/contracts/openapi.json` regenerated (`44f57f7`, a separate commit —
  the contract is a committed artifact and CI's drift gate fails without it).
- `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (+12) — a dedicated CI step for the backlink
  suites, whose comment states its own reasoning: *"The backlink module is the
  one shipping DARK: its routes are registered on the app but answer 404 until
  BACKLINKS_ENABLED flips. That makes a regression here invisible in production
  — nobody is exercising it — so the gate has to be the thing that notices."*

**PR #34 — the screens** (`6fd4f1b`). ~2,700 lines of frontend across 15 new
components plus three test files.

- `/backlinks` picks a project; `/backlinks/[projectId]` carries five tabs —
  Overview, Backlinks (server-side filter/sort/page), Referring domains, New &
  lost, Opportunities.
- Deliberately **no second create-project flow**: projects already exist in Site
  Audit, and per the commit message, *"two front doors to one entity is how a
  domain ends up with two projects and a split profile."*
- The stated hard part was not the tables but carrying the module's honesty
  rules to the surface, "because every one of them can be thrown away by a
  renderer": a null score renders as an em dash and never a confident `0`; an
  unmeasurable pull is labelled wherever its numbers appear, because "its
  zero-lost is a refusal to claim, not an observation of stability."
- `frontend/lib/shell-nav.ts` — the Backlinks entry graduates from `soon` to
  `live`. The comment defends the badge while `BACKLINKS_ENABLED` is off: the
  screens exist and say plainly that no index is connected, which "is a
  different statement from 'this feature does not exist', which is what 'soon'
  claimed."
